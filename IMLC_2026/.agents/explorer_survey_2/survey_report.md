### 2.2 Stage I: Qualification Round (Vòng Sơ loại) — Open Research & Derivation
- **Examination Character**: Untimed, take-home, open-book academic research. Participants are encouraged to consult textbooks, lecture notes, and peer-reviewed literature to produce rigorous, publication-grade solutions.
- **Problem Set**: Exactly 5 problems carrying 5.0 points each (total 25.0 points), covering key areas of machine learning.
- **Submission Rules**:
  - Exclusively via `https://imlco.org/submission`.
  - Format: Single PDF file (strongly recommended) or high-resolution images, strictly $\le 10.0$ MB.
  - Languages: English (primary), or supported languages on the IMLC portal (including Vietnamese).
  - Hard Deadline: **Sunday, 13 December 2026, 23:59 UTC+0**. Portal locks automatically upon deadline expiration.
- **Grading Pipeline**:
  1. *Automated Integrity Pre-Screening*: Heuristic parsing, optical character recognition, and cross-checking against plagiarism databases and AI boilerplate.
  2. *Double-Blind Academic Review*: Evaluation by human jury members who assign partial credit based on derivation steps, logical completeness, and proof rigor.
- **Results Release**: **Monday, 28 December 2026, 12:00 UTC+0**.

### 2.3 Stage II: Pre-Final Round (Vòng Bán kết) — The Research Paper Paradigm
The Pre-Final Round is the intellectual hallmark of IMLC. Instead of solving toy synthetic exercises, candidates engage with real-world scientific literature:
- **Phase 1 (Preparation & Literature Mining — 48 Hours)**:
  - Begins **Friday, 22 January 2027, 12:00 UTC+0**.
  - An authentic, peer-reviewed research paper from top venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, or major ArXiv preprint) is released on candidate dashboards.
  - Candidates have ~48 hours to read, analyze, re-derive mathematics, and dissect empirical findings with open access to any study resources.
- **Phase 2 (Supervised Timed Examination — 60 Minutes)**:
  - Exam window: **24 January (00:01 UTC+0) to 26 January 2027 (23:59 UTC+0)**.
  - Format: 3 problems totaling 18.0 points:
    - *Basic Problem (4 pts)*: Core machine learning foundations.
    - *Advanced Problem (6 pts)*: Continuous optimization, mathematical proof, or matrix calculus.
    - *Research Critique Problem (8 pts)*: Direct technical critique, boundary analysis, or proposed validation experiments for the assigned paper.
  - Supervision:
    - *Track A (In-Person Teacher Proctoring)*: Supervised by an accredited educator in a physical room.
    - *Track B (Dual-Camera Online Proctoring)*: Primary screen/webcam feed plus a secondary smartphone camera positioned diagonally behind the candidate capturing workspace, hands, and monitor.
- **Phase 3 (Digitization & Dynamic QR Upload — 10 Minutes)**:
  - As soon as the 60-minute exam timer ends, a dynamic session QR code appears on screen.
  - Candidate has strictly 10 minutes to photograph handwritten sheets with a smartphone and upload them to the server.
- **Results Release**: **Monday, 8 February 2027, 12:00 UTC+0**.

### 2.4 Stage III: Final Round (Vòng Chung kết) — Live Proctored Speed Sprint
The Final Round evaluates rapid intuition, mental deduction, and conceptual fluency under extreme time constraints:
- **Date**: **Tuesday, 23 February 2027** (Slot booking open 8–20 Feb 2027 across multiple global time zones).
- **Duration & Volume**: Strictly **40 minutes** for approximately **30 questions** (~75–80 seconds per question).
- **Question Types**: Rapid short-answer numerical/symbolic entries (1–2 pts) and 4-option multiple choice questions (1 pt).
- **Strict Non-Backtracking Engine**:
  - Each question has an independent countdown timer.
  - Once submitted or upon timer expiration, the question is permanently locked. **No returning to past questions.**
  - **No Negative Marking**: Candidates should never leave a question blank; an informed deduction is always advantageous.
- **Strict Materials Policy**: Handheld calculators and computational software are strictly prohibited. Candidates may use only blank scratch paper and a pen.
- **Results & Honours Ceremony**: **Monday, 1 March 2027, 12:00 UTC+0**.

### 4.2 Detailed Dimension Analysis

#### 1. IMLC vs. Kaggle Competitions
- **Methodological Divide**: Kaggle rewards empirical iterative experimentation, feature stacking, and ensembling (e.g., blending 20 LightGBM and XGBoost seeds with a CatBoost model). A participant can place in the top 1% on Kaggle without knowing the derivation of the Hessian in Newton-Raphson optimization or the spectral shrinkage factors in Ridge regression.
- **IMLC Distinction**: IMLC asks *why* a method works. It requires candidates to write down the penalization functional, compute the First-Order and Second-Order conditions, prove that the Hessian is strictly positive definite, and evaluate asymptotic limits. Kaggle produces skilled data tinkerers; IMLC produces foundational machine learning researchers.

#### 2. IMLC vs. IOI / ICPC (Competitive Programming)
- **Mathematical Space**: IOI and ICPC operate almost exclusively in discrete mathematics, combinatorics, graph theory, number theory, and dynamic programming. Continuous variables are typically rounded to integers or treated as geometry coordinates.
- **IMLC Distinction**: Modern machine learning is founded on continuous mathematics: differential calculus, continuous optimization, measure-theoretic probability, matrix manifolds, and information geometry. IMLC is the natural academic home for students who love calculus, optimization, and statistical theory rather than discrete graph traversal.

#### 3. IMLC vs. IOAI (International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence)
- **Format & Scale**: IOAI is an emerging, high-profile on-site international olympiad for high-school delegations featuring both a Practical Round (often involving hands-on Python/Jupyter tasks in computer vision, NLP, or RL) and a Scientific Round.
- **IMLC Distinction**: While IOAI focuses heavily on secondary-school national teams with intense on-site logistics, IMLC is a globally open, multi-division competition (with university/Senior students constituting its premier cohort). Moreover, IMLC introduces the unique **48-Hour Research Paper Paradigm (Stage II)**, directly training students in authentic academic literature deconstruction.

#### 4. IMLC vs. NeurIPS Competitions
- **Scope & Audience**: NeurIPS competitions are targeted primarily at research laboratories, corporate AI teams, and doctoral researchers with substantial compute resources.
- **IMLC Distinction**: IMLC adapts the intellectual caliber of NeurIPS/ICML research into an accessible, hardware-agnostic competition for ambitious students. It tests the foundational principles underpinning NeurIPS papers without requiring $10,000 in GPU cloud credits.

1. **Pillar 1: Core Machine Learning Methods**:

   - Kernel Methods: Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS), Mercer’s theorem, SVM Wolfe dual formulation.

2. **Pillar 2: Optimization Theory & Dynamics**:
   - First-Order Optimization: Convergence rates of Gradient Descent, Polyak Momentum, Nesterov Acceleration, RMSprop, Adam moment bias correction.
   - Decoupled Weight Decay: Loshchilov & Hutter (AdamW) proof demonstrating why $L_2$ regularization in adaptive optimizers fails to execute true parameter shrinkage.
   - Convex Optimization: Convex sets, epigraphs, subgradient calculus, Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) stationarity and complementary slackness conditions.
   - Regularization Geometry: Laplace ($L_1$ Lasso) coordinate sparsity vs. Gaussian ($L_2$ Ridge) spectral shrinkage; closed-form normal equations $w^* = (X^T X + \lambda I)^{-1} X^T y$.

3. **Pillar 3: Deep Learning Formulations & Architectures**:
   - Representational Capacity: Cybenko and Hornik Universal Approximation Theorems.

4. **Pillar 4: Frontier Models & RLHF Alignment**:
   - Language Modeling: Autoregressive token generation, temperature scaling, top-$p$ / top-$k$ sampling.
   - Preference Optimization: Bradley-Terry preference probability, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) clipped surrogate loss, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) closed-form reparameterization.
   - Information Geometry & Drift: Reverse KL divergence ($\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\log(\pi / \pi_{\text{ref}})]$) mode-seeking behavior; derivation of safe boundary thresholds bounding policy drift away from an uncorrupted anchor.

5. **Pillar 5: Real-World Applications & MLOps**:
   - Lifecycle Engineering: Continuous deployment, automated data validation, feedback loops.
   - Quantization Mechanics: Affine integer quantization arithmetic ($X_{\text{quant}} = \text{clip}(\text{round}(X/S) + Z)$), Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) vs. Quantization-Aware Training (QAT).
   - Distribution Shift Detection: Mathematical distinctions between Covariate Shift ($P(X)$ changes), Concept Shift ($P(Y|X)$ changes), and Prior Shift ($P(Y)$ changes); non-parametric testing via Two-Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov ($D = \sup_x |F_1(x) - F_2(x)|$) and Population Stability Index (PSI).

6. **Pillar 6: Trustworthy AI, Safety & Governance**:
   - Algorithmic Fairness: Demographic Parity, Equal Opportunity, Equalized Odds; proof of **Kleinberg’s Impossibility Theorem** (Calibration, Balance for Positive Class, and Balance for Negative Class cannot simultaneously hold except under identical base rates or perfect accuracy).
   - Adversarial Robustness: Min-max saddle-point formulations, Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD).
   - Governance & Safety Architectures: Grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) validation pipelines, Hallucination mitigation, Human-in-the-Loop escalation, and international risk classifications (e.g., EU AI Act high-risk requirements).

### 6.2 Round-by-Round Time Budgeting & Operational Tactics

#### Stage I (Qualification Round — Take-Home)
- **Time Allocation**: Spend approximately **10 to 12 focused hours per problem** using a 4-draft refinement cycle:
  - *Draft 1 (Scratchpad Derivation & Python Prototyping)*: Derive all formulas by hand; verify calculations in standalone Python scripts.
  - *Draft 2 (Mathematical Formalization)*: Frame solutions with formal Definition, Lemma, Theorem, and Proof structures. State First-Order AND Second-Order conditions explicitly.
  - *Draft 3 (Digital Typesetting in LaTeX)*: Typeset with professional typography (`amsmath`, `booktabs`), generate native TikZ graphics for trees/architectures, and assemble a formal BibTeX bibliography to earn the **Special Honour for Digital Submission**.
  - *Draft 4 (Rubric Self-Audit)*: Audit solutions against the 4-tier Senior rubric. Verify PDF size is under 10.0 MB.

#### Stage II (Pre-Final Round — 60-Minute Exam)
Every minute must be allocated with precision:
- **Minute 00 – 12 (12m)**: *Basic Problem (4 pts)*. Rapidly solve foundational theory. Write clean, complete equations. Target: 4.0 / 4.0.
- **Minute 12 – 30 (18m)**: *Advanced Problem (6 pts)*. Formulate calculus, optimization, or proofs. State FOC and SOC. Target: $\ge 5.0 / 6.0$.
- **Minute 30 – 55 (25m)**: *Research Critique Problem (8 pts)*. Interrogate the assigned paper directly. Cite equations and sections. Detail unstated assumptions and propose falsification tests. Target: $\ge 6.5 / 8.0$.
- **Minute 55 – 60 (5m)**: *Review Buffer*. Check completeness, candidate ID, and equation numbering.
- **Next 10 Minutes**: *Dynamic QR Scan Phase*. Photograph answer pages sequentially, inspect upload thumbnails, and secure server confirmation token.

#### Stage III (Final Round — 40-Minute Sprint)
Under the **Strictly Non-Backtracking** engine, candidates must execute instant triage upon encountering each question:
- **Tier 1 (Fast Strike — 45–60 seconds)**: Direct definitions, basic matrix dimensions, simple single-variable derivatives. Compute and lock in points immediately.
- **Tier 2 (Calculated Reasoning — 75–90 seconds)**: 2-step algebraic manipulations, fast mental approximations (Taylor series, sigmoid bounds).
- **Tier 3 (Strategic Estimation — 100–120 seconds)**: Complex derivations. Apply dimensional analysis, asymptotic bounding, and eliminate impossible options.
- **Golden Rule**: **Never leave a question blank**. Because there is no negative grading penalty, an intelligent deduction is strictly superior to letting the question timer expire without an answer.

### 6.3 Mental Math & Estimation Techniques (No-Calculator Sprint Survival)
Because handheld calculators are strictly prohibited in the Final Round, candidates must develop mental estimation techniques:
1. **Natural Logarithm & Power-of-Two Anchors**:
   - $\ln(2) \approx 0.693 \approx 0.70$
   - $\ln(10) \approx 2.302 \approx 2.30$
   - $\log_2(x) = \frac{\ln(x)}{\ln(2)} \approx 1.443 \ln(x)$
2. **First-Order Taylor Approximations (for small $|x| \ll 1$)**:
   - $\ln(1 + x) \approx x - \frac{x^2}{2}$
   - $e^x \approx 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2}$
   - $(1 + x)^p \approx 1 + px$
3. **Sigmoid $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$ Key Anchor Points**:
   - $\sigma(0) = 0.50$
   - $\sigma(1) \approx 0.731$
   - $\sigma(2) \approx 0.881$
   - $\sigma(3) \approx 0.953$
   - Linear approximation near origin: $\sigma(z) \approx 0.5 + 0.25z$ for $|z| \le 1$.
4. **Information Impurity Approximations for Binary Splits ($p \in [0, 1]$)**:
   - For balanced split ($p = 0.5$): Entropy $H = 1.0\text{ bit}$, Gini $= 0.50$.
   - For skewed split ($p = 0.2$): Gini $= 2(0.2)(0.8) = 0.32$, Entropy $H \approx 0.72\text{ bit}$.
   - Rule of Thumb: Near extreme boundaries, Entropy $H(p) \approx 2 \times \text{Gini}(p)$.
5. **Dimensional & Matrix Sanity Checks**:
   - Trace invariance: $\text{Tr}(AB) = \text{Tr}(BA)$.
   - Check output dimensions: For feature matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ and parameter vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^d$, immediately classify expressions: $w^T X^T X w \in \mathbb{R}$ (scalar), $X^T y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (vector), $X^T X \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ (square matrix). Dimensional incompatibility eliminates incorrect multiple-choice options in seconds.
