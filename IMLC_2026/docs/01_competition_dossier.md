#### Submission Protocol & Formatting Mandate
- **Submission Channel**: Submissions are accepted exclusively through the official digital portal at `https://imlco.org/submission`. Email attachments or physical postal mailings are strictly rejected.

#### Proctoring Protocols & Supervision Framework
Candidates must elect one of two strictly regulated supervision protocols:
1. **Track A: Teacher / Faculty In-Person Supervision (Recommended)**:
   - The candidate sits for the examination under the physical supervision of an accredited secondary school teacher or university faculty member (who must not be an immediate relative).
   - The teacher verifies the candidate's identity, inspects the physical workspace, monitors the 60-minute duration, and submits photographic confirmation of the physical examination environment via the proctor portal.
2. **Track B: IMLC Dual-Camera Online Supervision**:
   - Compulsory for candidates unable to arrange in-person teacher proctoring.
   - Requires two concurrent camera feeds:
     - **Device 1 (Primary Workspace)**: Laptop or desktop computer running the examination browser, equipped with an active forward-facing webcam and full-screen proctoring lock.
     - **Device 2 (Secondary Observer)**: Smartphone or tablet with an active camera and microphone positioned at a rear diagonal angle (approximately 45 degrees, 1.5–2.0 meters away), clearly capturing the candidate's torso, hands, physical writing desk, and computer monitor throughout the entire 60 minutes.

#### Authorized & Prohibited Examination Materials
- **Authorized**: Up to 20 sheets of clean, blank white paper; up to 3 writing pens; standard scientific handheld calculator; drinking water.
- **Prohibited**: External textbooks, physical paper copies of the research paper (the paper is provided natively within the digital exam interface), pre-written summary sheets, smartwatches, and secondary browser tabs.

### 2.4 Stage III: Final Round (Vòng Chung kết) — Live Proctored Examination

#### Examination Paradigm: The 40-Minute High-Speed Live Sprint
The Final Round is a live, high-intensity proctored examination designed to evaluate conceptual fluency, rapid mathematical deduction, and deep intuition under strict time pressure.

- **Official Examination Date**: **Tuesday, 23 February 2027**.
- **Time Slot Reservation**: Finalists must pre-book their specific examination slot between **8 February and 20 February 2027** at `https://imlco.org/schedule-exam`. Examination slots are distributed across multiple global windows to accommodate evening hours in local time zones worldwide.
- **Duration**: Strictly **40 minutes**.
- **Question Volume**: Approximately **30 sequential questions** spanning the entire 6-pillar Senior curriculum, featuring questions synthesized from the Qualification and Pre-Final research contexts.

#### Question Formats & Non-Backtracking Pacing Engine
1. **Short-Answer Questions (1.0 – 2.0 Points; 60–120 Seconds Target)**:
   - Candidates must compute and enter an exact numerical value, matrix dimension, closed-form expression, or precise academic terminology.
2. **Multiple-Choice Questions (1.0 Point; 60 Seconds Target)**:
   - High-discrimination four-option questions evaluating edge-case understanding and theoretical failure modes.

$$\mathbf{CRITICAL\ PACING\ RULE:\ NO\ BACKTRACKING}$$
- Each question appears individually on screen accompanied by an autonomous question-level countdown timer.
- Once a candidate submits an answer or the individual timer expires, the question is permanently locked. **Candidates cannot return to previous questions under any circumstances.**
- **No Negative Marking**: Incorrect responses incur zero points; no penalty points are deducted.

#### Final Round Proctoring & Environment Security
- **Proctoring Engine**: 100% automated live online supervision administered through the secure IMLC Proctored Testing Client. (Teacher proctoring is **not permitted** in the Final Round to maintain absolute global parity).
- **Authorized Materials**: Blank scratch paper and a writing pen only.
- **Strictly Prohibited**: Handheld calculators, computational software, notes, external monitors, books, or external communications.

#### Hierarchy of Tie-Breaking Rules
In the event that two or more candidates achieve identical total point scores in the Final Round, official world rankings and cash prize distributions are resolved using the following strict hierarchy:
1. **Primary Metric**: Total Final Round Examination Score.
2. **Secondary Tie-Breaker**: Total Pre-Final Round Examination Score (out of 18.0).
3. **Tertiary Tie-Breaker**: Total Qualification Round Score (out of 25.0).
4. **Quaternary Tie-Breaker**: Time-efficiency metric recorded by the Final Round pacing engine.

- **Global Awards & Winners Announcement**: **Monday, 1 March 2027**.

### 3.2 Senior Division Eligibility Criteria & Target Profile
A candidate is officially classified within the **Senior Division** if they satisfy either of the following conditions:
1. **Academic Enrollment**: The candidate is actively enrolled in an accredited undergraduate degree program (e.g., B.Sc., B.Eng., B.A.), vocational higher education institution, or early-stage graduate degree program (e.g., first-year M.Sc.).
2. **Chronological Age**: The candidate has attained **19 years of age or older** ($\text{Age} \ge 19$) on or before the official Qualification Round deadline.

There is no formal upper age limit for the Senior Division, provided the candidate meets the non-faculty, non-senior-industry-researcher student-learner status defined in competition bylaws.

---

### 3.3 The Qualification Deadline Age Freezing Rule
A foundational administrative policy of IMLC is the **Age Freezing Rule**:
$$\mathbf{Age\ Freezing\ Date:\ Sunday,\ 13\ December\ 2026,\ 23:59\ UTC+0}$$

- **Temporal Invariance**: A candidate's division eligibility is permanently frozen based on their exact age or academic status as of the Qualification Round deadline.
- **Birthday Immunity**: If a candidate is 18 years old on 13 December 2026 and subsequently celebrates their 19th birthday in January 2027, they **remain in the Youth Division** for the entire 2026–2027 competition season (including the Pre-Final and Final rounds).
- **The Gap-Year Stipulation**:
  - Candidates taking a formal "gap year" between secondary school and university who are under 19 years old on 13 December 2026 are classified in the **Youth Division**.
  - Candidates on a gap year who are 19 years or older on 13 December 2026 are strictly classified in the **Senior Division**.

---

### 3.4 Universal Problem Set vs. Differential Evaluation Standards
IMLC utilizes a unified academic examination model:
- **Identical Problem Sets**: All candidates across Junior, Youth, and Senior divisions receive the exact same problem papers in the Qualification Round (Problems A–E), Pre-Final Round, and Final Round.
- **Stratified Evaluation Rigor**: Parity is achieved through differentiated scoring thresholds and distinct rubric expectations. While a Junior candidate can qualify for the Pre-Final with 12 points, a Senior candidate must achieve at least **17 points**. Furthermore, Senior submissions are evaluated with the analytical rigor expected of peer-reviewed scientific manuscripts.

---

### 3.5 Participant Verification & Academic Credential Protocols
To ensure the integrity of international awards and cash prize disbursements, all candidates advancing to the Pre-Final and Final rounds must complete mandatory identity verification:
1. **Government Identity Verification**: Submission of an official government-issued photo ID (National Identity Card, Passport, or Driver's License) validating full legal name and date of birth.
2. **Academic Status Documentation**: Submission of a valid University Student Identification Card, official enrollment certificate, or tuition confirmation letter validating student status.
3. **Institutional Disqualification Safeguards**: Falsification of age or enrollment credentials results in immediate global disqualification, forfeiture of all awards and certificates, and notification of the home university's academic integrity board.

### 4.3 Problem-Specific Rubric Applications (Evaluation Criteria across Topics A–E)

#### Problem A: Machine Learning Lifecycle & Continuous Learning
- **Full Credit (5.0 pts)**:
  - Formulates Mitchell's classic learning definition $\langle T, P, E \rangle$ precisely mapped to agricultural climate telemetry.
  - Distinguishes Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) on offline training sets from expected generalization risk on out-of-distribution environments.
  - Formulates continuous retraining mechanics and mathematically defines non-stationary concept drift ($P_{t_1}(Y|X) \ne P_{t_2}(Y|X)$) as distinct from covariate shift ($P_{t_1}(X) \ne P_{t_2}(X)$).

#### Problem B: The Greenhouse Tree & Deterministic Logic
- **Full Credit (5.0 pts)**:
  - Correctly applies decision tree splitting rules to evaluate query feature vectors.
  - Identifies training sample inconsistencies against deterministic tree partitioning.
  - Demonstrates information gain / Gini impurity optimization to resolve classification errors and constructs an optimal tree topology with zero training error.
  - Provides a clean, publication-grade decision tree diagram illustrating condition nodes and terminal leaves.

#### Problem C: To Fit or Not to Fit (L2 Ridge Regularization)
- **Full Credit (5.0 pts)**:
  - Formulates the exact penalized objective:
    $$J(w) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left(y_i - f(x_i; w)\right)^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^d w_j^2$$
  - Correctly separates empirical training error from the parameter shrinkage penalty term across competing model architectures.
  - Demonstrates how the structural regularization parameter $\lambda$ mediates model complexity selection.
  - Provides formal Bias-Variance tradeoff rationale demonstrating why the structural penalty suppresses high-frequency polynomial variance.

#### Problem D: The Price of Drift (RLHF Alignment & Safety Boundary)
- **Full Credit (5.0 pts)**:
  - Formulates the stationary point condition for regularized policy drift optimization.
  - Proves strict convexity via second derivative, establishing global optimality.
  - Evaluates asymptotic qualitative behavior as the regularization strength approaches extreme boundaries ($\beta \to 0^+$ vs. $\beta \to \infty$).
  - Establishes safe regularization boundaries to guarantee that worst-case drift does not exceed pre-specified trust-region limits.
  - Synthesizes the cross-paradigm equivalence between quadratic drift penalties and L2 parameter shrinkage priors.

#### Problem E: Agricultural LLM Deployment (Trustworthy AI in Low-Resource Domains)
- **Full Credit (5.0 pts)**:
  - Identifies core opportunities for generative AI in low-resource, agrarian domains (e.g., multilingual extension services, localized pest diagnosis).
  - Analyzes critical high-consequence failure modes: hallucinated agrochemical dosages, dialect translation degradation, and ungrounded advice.
  - Formulates a production-grade trustworthy architecture featuring Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), conformal prediction for calibrated abstention, and human-in-the-loop extension workflows.
